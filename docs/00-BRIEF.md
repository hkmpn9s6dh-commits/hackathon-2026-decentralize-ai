# 00 — BRIEF

Competition: **Decentralize AI Hackathon by HackerNoon** — https://decentralizeai.tech/
Read on 2026-11-13. Sources: the hackathon site, its judges page, and the open-source site repo
(`github.com/hackernoon/decentralize-ai-hackathon`).

## What the competition actually asks for

> Publish a blog post on HackerNoon, with any of the listed tags, tackling at least one layer of
> open AI infrastructure — decentralized GPU orchestration, permanent model storage, **verifiable
> AI**, open inference, or data sovereignty. Concept-stage ideas with clear technical direction
> are just as welcome as shipped products.

Rounds: Round 1 ran 2026-06 → 2026-10 (closed). **Round 2 runs 2026-11 → 2027-02-27.** Half the
prize pool is awarded in each round. Entries are unlimited — "enter as many times as you want".

## How it is judged

Two stated criteria, quoted verbatim because they are the only rubric we get:

1. **"Bring fresh perspectives that challenge conventional thinking. Don't rehash what everyone
   already knows."**
2. **"Address actual problems with actionable solutions. Theory is great; impact is better."**

Process: entries are shortlisted on readership and quality, then the panel votes. Panel includes
David Smooke (HackerNoon), Linh Dao Smooke, Sjoerd Dijkstra (Nosana), Miko Matsumura (Gumi
Cryptos), Saumya Tyagi (Microsoft), Ojus Save (Render), Valeria Kholostenko (Decentralized
Storage Alliance) and others.

## Prizes

$51,750+ across **15 winners**. Separately, the first 500 participants can claim $70 of Nosana
compute credits, and there is a $450 pool for follow-up posts that show what you built with them.

## What that means for this entry

- The deliverable is **a written argument**, not a product page. A working demo is evidence, not
  the submission.
- "Don't rehash" rules out another "zkML will fix trust" essay. The argument has to be specific
  enough to be falsifiable and cheap enough that a reader could adopt it on Monday.
- Readership matters, so the piece has to be readable by a developer who has never thought about
  attestation — not by a cryptographer.
- Nothing here rewards scale. The honest move is to pick the smallest real gap and close it
  completely.

## Constraints we accepted

- No API keys, no paid services, no accounts beyond GitHub (build) and HackerNoon (publish).
- The demo must run offline with no network calls, so a reader can clone it and get the same bytes.
- Every claim in the article must be reproducible from the repo, or be labelled as an opinion.
